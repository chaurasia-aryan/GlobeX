# Source Verification Notes

This file records external-source facts used to design the compliance architecture.

## OFAC

OFAC states sanctions programs may be comprehensive or selective and does not maintain one universal country list. It also states that designated persons can be blocked regardless of location and that ownership rules can extend blocking to entities owned by blocked persons. Source: OFAC Sanctions Programs / Country Information and related guidance.

## UN

The UN Security Council publishes a consolidated sanctions list with regime-specific measures and current XML/HTML/PDF versions.

## BIS

BIS export controls include destination, end-use and end-user controls. The Consolidated Screening List combines multiple US government restricted-party lists and is intended as a screening aid.

## EU

The European Commission maintains official restrictive-measures resources, including the consolidated financial sanctions list and sanctions map.

## UK

The UK Sanctions List is the current source for UK sanctions designations from 28 January 2026; the former OFSI Consolidated List is no longer updated.

## India

DGFT materials distinguish Free, Restricted, Prohibited and State Trading Enterprise export categories and state that other laws can impose additional conditions. India's Foreign Trade Policy also recognizes product-specific restrictions, NOCs and statutory compliance requirements.

These notes are architecture references, not legal advice. The application must retrieve the current primary rule before making a transaction-specific decision.
